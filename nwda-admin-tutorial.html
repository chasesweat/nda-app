The Admin Playbook
Everything you can do that regular drivers can’t — assigning rides, managing the team, approving requests, controlling client lists, and pulling the reports that keep the business honest. This guide is admin-only; don’t share the link with drivers.
Admins only
★
Who counts as an admin
Kat, Chase, and Morgan. Admins see extra buttons and panels throughout the app that are invisible to drivers.
Tip: tap the ☰ menu (bottom-left) any time to jump straight to a topic.
Rides
Assign & Manage Any Ride
Drivers can only touch rides they created. Admins can touch every ride on the calendar.
Admin power
1
Assign a driver to any ride
Open any ride card and use the Driver dropdown → Assign. The driver gets a notification. Drivers can only assign rides they personally created — you can assign all of them.
2
Reassign or unassign
Change the driver any time. If the new driver isn’t available at that time, the app warns you and lets you override.
3
Cancel any ride
Admins can cancel any ride on the calendar, not just their own.
4
See who last touched a ride
Every ride keeps a history trail. Under the Created by line in the ride detail you can see who last edited, assigned, or changed the status, with a timestamp, and tap View full history for the complete log. Useful for sorting out who changed what.
5
🔒 Private notes stay with the creator
Each ride has a Private notes box visible only to the ride’s creator — not other drivers, and not even admins. The one exception is Kat and Morgan, who share private notes since Morgan dispatches on Kat’s behalf.
6
👥 Kat & Morgan share one client list
Anything Morgan enters — a new client, an edit, a client saved from a booking — lands on Kat’s client list automatically. Morgan never has a separate list, and any older entries that were stored under Morgan have been merged into Kat’s list (duplicates combined by name).
7
📧 Confirmations follow the client owner
Pickup instructions and the day-of selfie always come from whoever owns the client — not the assigned driver, not whoever hits send. Kat’s client with Chase driving still gets Kat’s instructions and selfie (Morgan’s clients count as Kat’s). And if a client has no phone number on file anywhere, the app now warns loudly before a group text opens, so a confirmation can’t silently go out without the client.
8
➕ Add a driver
On the Team page, tap Add a driver (admins only). Enter their name, a login username and temporary password, phone, vehicle, a card color, and upload their driver card image. They can log in right away and finish the rest themselves under My Profile. The new driver is added everywhere at once — cards, calendar, client list, fees — and sticks across reloads.
9
🪪 Profiles moved to My Profile
Each driver now edits their own info in the My Profile menu tab — vehicle, home address, gas/MPG, Venmo, confirmation text, selfie, availability, and which parts their “Send everything” text includes. The Team page shows public, shareable cards only. First login of this version sends each driver straight to My Profile to set up.
Editing note: the Edit button still follows creator rules — only the driver who created a ride can change its details. Assigning and cancelling are the admin-wide powers.
The Team
Manage Drivers
The Drivers page is your team control panel. Admins can edit every driver’s profile and settings.
Admin power
1
Edit any driver’s profile
Update contact info, vehicle, photo, and the home address used for mileage routing. Drivers can only edit their own; you can edit all.
2
Set availability & days off
Mark weekly days off, add unavailable hours, or block specific dates for any driver. The app uses this to warn before double-booking.
3
Enable or disable access
Disable a driver to revoke their login without deleting their history. Re-enable any time.
4
Cost settings are private
Each driver’s personal cost settings — home address, gas price, MPG, and maintenance rate — are private to that driver. Admins cannot see or edit another driver’s cost numbers; you only manage your own.
Approvals
Client Transfers & Releases
When a driver wants to hand a client to someone else — or release one back to the pool — it comes to the admins to approve.
Admin approval
1
Where requests show up
Pending transfer and release requests appear in your notification bell. Drivers don’t see each other’s requests — only admins (and the driver involved) do.
2
Approve or deny
Open the request and choose. Approving moves the client to the new driver; denying leaves things as they were and notifies the requester.
3
Admin-initiated transfers
You can also start a transfer or release yourself from a client’s page, without waiting for a driver to ask.
4
⋯ The client ⋯ menu
Release and transfer live on each client’s ⋯ button in the directory, alongside three more actions: Mark cautious (the shared team list), plus Mark deceased and Remove — both private to you, so they only change your own list and no other driver or admin can see them.
Client Privacy
Unlock a Driver’s Client List
Each driver’s client list is private. To view another driver’s list, an admin needs a second admin’s approval — a deliberate two-key safeguard.
Double approvalSensitive
1
Request access
Go to Team → Driver Cards and tap 🔒 Unlock a driver’s client list — admins only, so drivers never see this button. Pick the driver whose list you need and send the request. You count as the first approval automatically.
2
A different admin approves
Every other admin gets a notification. A second, different admin must approve — you can’t approve your own request.
3
24-hour access
Once approved, a dropdown on the Client Directory lets you switch to that driver’s list. Access auto-expires after 24 hours and can be revoked any time.
Client Privacy
Contact Info Is Locked Down
Client phone numbers are some of the most sensitive data in the app. Even admins don’t get a free pass.
Privacy by design
1
Who sees a phone number
Only the ride’s creator and the assigned driver. This holds in the ride card, the calendar search, and everywhere else — being an admin does not reveal numbers on rides you’re not part of.
2
The client list unlock is the path
If you genuinely need another driver’s client contacts, that’s what the double-admin Unlock List flow is for — it’s logged and time-limited on purpose.
This protects the drivers’ books of business and keeps client trust intact. It’s a feature, not a limitation.
Money
How Pricing & Fees Work
The exact rules the app uses to price a ride and to figure the 15% fee. Knowing these makes the Pricing Audit easy to read and helps you sanity-check any quote.
Pricing rules
1
Airport runs — base + mileage over a 2-mile buffer
AP Run / AP Pick Up / AP Round Trip start at the vehicle base rate (Sedan $105, SUV/Van $145, Premium $185) which includes the first 50 miles. There’s a 2-mile grace buffer: trips up to 52 miles are just the base rate. Once a trip goes past 52 miles, the miles over 50 are charged at $2.50 each. Example (Sedan): 52 mi = $105, 53 mi = $112.50, 56 mi = $120.
2
Round Trip — total_miles × $2.50 + $20
Enter the one-way distance; the app doubles it for the round trip. Price = (one-way miles × 2) × $2.50 + $20 + any vehicle upcharge. The $20 is the 2 base pickups ($10 each, there & back) — already included, so don’t add extra pickups for them.
3
One Way / Local — miles × $2.50 + $10
Price = miles × $2.50 + $10 + any vehicle upcharge. The $10 is the 1 base pickup. When you pick Round Trip or One Way in the quote screen, a blue note reminds you the base pickup(s) are already priced in. This line shows on the breakdown as Pickup/Drop-off fee.
4
Extra pickups & wait time — type a number
Additional pickups are $10 each and wait time is $60/hour. You can type the number directly (e.g. 3 pickups, 6 hours) or use the +/− buttons. Only add pickups beyond the base one(s) already in the price.
5
Surcharges auto-fill, but you can override
Toggling a surcharge (premium, holiday, Christmas, late, traffic, fuel) fills the amount with the correct total — base + mileage overage + surcharges, with fuel’s 8% on the whole thing. You can always type a custom amount over it; the Pricing Audit will simply flag it as a manual adjustment.
6
The 15% fee — who owes it
When a driver drives someone else’s client, they owe the client’s owner 15% of the fare (never the tip). Morgan’s clients count as Kat’s. If you own the client and drive it yourself, no fee. In the driver’s income, the 15% is subtracted from their take-home for that ride.
7
No fee when the client is a driver
If the ride’s client is themselves a driver in the group (e.g. a ride booked for Morgan Newton), no 15% is ever owed — no matter who drives it. The app recognizes this automatically everywhere the fee is shown.
8
Driven miles never change the client’s price
When a driver logs the miles they actually drove (Log Miles or GPS), that number is used only for their gas/maintenance and profit. It is tracked separately and never changes the fare the client was quoted. The client fare is always the mileage entered on the quote.
9
Fix fare miles in Edit Fee
The Edit Fee panel has a Trip miles (for the fare) field on AP rides. Change it and the suggested total recalculates live (e.g. correcting 114 → 56 miles drops $265 → $120). This is how you hand-correct any ride whose fare mileage is wrong — it never touches the driver’s logged driving miles.
These are the standard formulas. A scheduler can always override the final price by typing an amount — the Pricing Audit’s variance column is how you spot those.
July update
Payments & Airport Routing
One combined paid state, automatic unpaid-ride carryover warnings, and exact-GPS terminal routing.
New
1
Completed & Paid is one state
The ride lifecycle is now Scheduled → Confirmed → Completed & Paid. The final stage means the ride is done and the client’s fare is collected. Older rides marked “completed” display as Completed & Paid and still count in income. This is only about the client’s fare — the 15% between-driver tracker stays separate.
2
Unpaid carryover banner
If a client’s most recent airport ride is completed but unpaid, their next airport ride shows a red banner naming the previous driver and what they’re owed, plus a display-only summary: amount for this ride, amount owed to the previous driver, and the total. It never changes the ride’s actual price — it’s a collection reminder. Visible to the client’s owner and the assigned driver.
3
Who marks what paid
When the second-leg driver collects and marks their ride paid, the previous ride stays unpaid. The previous driver and the client’s owner get an in-app + push notification that the money’s been collected; the previous driver marks their own ride paid once they receive it. No double-notifications if the paid button is toggled.
4
Exact terminal GPS pins
Start Ride routes airport rides to precise GPS coordinates for each terminal pickup spot (North Domestic, South Domestic, International) — verified on the ground — instead of relying on the airport’s street address. Applies to AP Run, AP Pick Up, and AP Round Trip; if no terminal is set on the ride, it falls back to the saved address.
5
Dashboard NET is real math now
The dashboard’s NET mirrors the Income page: gross minus vehicle cost (driven miles × the driver’s cost per mile) minus any 15% fees owed, minus monthly expenses. If NET looks too high for a driver, check that their MPG and gas price are set in My Profile — that powers the cost math.
Everything here is display / workflow — none of it changes what a client is quoted. Pricing formulas are on the previous slide.
Reports
Pricing Audit Report
The single most useful admin report. It breaks down every ride’s price into its parts and flags anything that doesn’t match the standard formula.
Admin report
1
Where to find it
On the Income page, tap 📋 Pricing Audit (admins only). It respects the date range you’ve selected — This Week, Month, Custom, or All Time.
2
What’s in it
A CSV with each ride broken into base rate, mileage, service fee, every surcharge, manual adjustments, and the final price — plus column totals.
3
The variance column is the gold
It compares the saved price to what the standard formula should produce. Any non-zero variance flags a manual override, a legacy price, or an admin-set rate — so you can spot pricing drift instantly.
Opens in Excel, Google Sheets, or Numbers. A $0.00 variance means the ride was priced exactly by the book.
Reports
Income & Year-End P&L
Your income is private to you. Like every driver, you only see your own earnings (plus any partner you have mutually linked with), along with the tools to close out your year.
Admin view
1
Your own income only
The Income page shows only your own rides and earnings — admins included. There is no fleet-wide or per-driver income view. If you have mutually linked profiles with a partner, you can optionally combine the two of you, but no one else’s numbers are ever visible.
2
Export the full CSV
Export CSV pulls your income detail for the selected range.
3
Year-End P&L spreadsheet
📊 Year-End P&L builds a formatted Excel workbook with your profit-and-loss for tax time.

Safety
The Cautious List
A shared heads-up list for clients the team should approach carefully. Admins curate it.
Team safety
1
Add an entry
Flag a client with a note so every driver sees the caution before accepting a ride.
2
Remove entries
Admins can remove any entry; drivers can only remove ones they added. Keep it current and fair.
Use this for genuine safety and reliability concerns — it’s visible to the whole team, so keep notes factual and professional.
Oversight
What Lands in Your Bell
Admins receive a wider set of notifications because more decisions route through you.
Admin view
🔔
Transfer & release requests
Every pending client transfer or release waiting on a decision.
🔔
Client-list unlock requests
When another admin asks to view a driver’s list, you’re notified so you can be the second approver.
🔔
Profile merge & override requests
Availability overrides and profile-merge requests surface to admins for a decision.
🔔
Clearing your bell
Opening the panel clears the red count, and read items appear dimmed. When the list gets long, tap Clear all at the top to empty it. Notifications don’t vanish on their own, so clear them when you want a fresh slate.
Opening the bell marks ride notifications as read. Action items like transfer requests stay until you actually approve or deny them — that’s intentional, so nothing slips through.
